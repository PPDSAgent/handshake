# Unit 06 — Questions & Grammar I

## Why this unit

Every unit so far has been declarative — you sign what you already know; real
conversation runs on the opposite move, where you don't know something, so you ask.
WHO, WHAT, WHERE, WHEN, WHY, and HOW are the words that turn a monologue into a
back-and-forth with the people you're doing this for. This unit also introduces the
fact that a question in ASL isn't just a WH-word produced with your hands — the
grammar shows up on your face too, and treating that as optional is the fastest way
to be read as spelling English with your hands instead of signing a different
language (Rule 6). You won't learn to produce that grammar from these pages; you'll
watch it, because that's the only honest way this tutor teaches it.

## Watch

- https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-layout/whfacialexpression.htm — names the
  specific non-manual marker that goes with WHO/WHAT/WHERE/WHEN/WHY/HOW questions,
  and why it isn't optional decoration (~3 min read).
- https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-layout/nonmanualmarkers.htm — the general
  concept: ASL questions are inflected on the face the way spoken English is
  inflected in the voice, and yes/no questions get a different marker than WH-word
  questions (~5 min read).
- https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/topics/sentencetypes.htm — walks through how the
  same non-manual grammar distinguishes questions from statements and other
  sentence types, with worked examples (~8 min read).
- https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/topics/nonmanual-signs-versus-nonmanual-markers.htm
  — a related distinction worth having straight: not everything your face or head
  does is grammar-marking; some of it is just another sign that happens to use the
  face instead of the hands (~6 min read).
- https://www.youtube.com/billvicars — Dr. Vicars' channel; search "WH question ASL"
  and watch one lesson video that shows a WH-question signed at natural speed. Seeing
  it live is what makes the concept pages click (~10 min).

## Drill

When this unit opens, `/lesson` appends to the deck:

- One `vocab` card per Learn row above: "Sign: WHO", "Sign: WHAT", "Sign: WHERE",
  "Sign: WHEN", "Sign: WHY", "Sign: HOW", "Sign: UNDERSTAND", "Sign: KNOW",
  "Sign: QUESTION" — source = each sign's link above. 9 cards.

Non-card practice, every session in this unit: rewatch one of the non-manual-marker
Watch links above (rotate through them across sessions), then the tutor asks what
changed on screen between the question and an ordinary statement — a Socratic
prompt about what was just watched, never an assertion from the tutor's memory
(Rule 1). Log the one-line answer in the journal.

## Family mission

Ask another person three WH-questions from this unit's vocabulary — mix it up
(WHO/WHAT/WHERE/WHEN/WHY/HOW), about anything: their day, dinner plans, whatever's
on hand. Let them answer however they can — sign, gesture, speech, write it down.
Note in your journal which question, if any, needed a repeat before it landed.
Under 10 minutes.

Learning with a partner? `learners/shared/missions.md` has the two-person version of
this and every other unit's mission.

# Unit 08 — Food & Restaurants

## Why this unit

Meals are where conversations actually happen — at a family table, ordering out, or
checking in on whether someone's eaten yet. This unit gives you the vocabulary for
that: what you want to drink, what you're ordering, whether you're done. It's also
the first unit where a single sign, FINISH, does double duty as both a word and a
piece of grammar — that payoff shows up again once you hit tense and aspect later in
the curriculum.

## Drill

When this unit opens, `/lesson` appends to the deck:

- One `vocab` card per Learn row above (11 cards; prompts "Sign: WATER" … "Sign:
  FINISH"), source = each sign's Lifeprint page.

No `phrase` cards this unit. An earlier draft paired FINISH with a state (HUNGRY
FINISH) and a noun (ME WATER FINISH) to drill it as a completion marker — but no
allowlist source shows FINISH used that way. Every example on FINISH's own source
page pairs it with a verb ("YOU FINISH HOMEWORK YOU?", "TODAY YOU FINISH
BRUSH-TEETH YOU?"), and this unit doesn't yet teach a verb sign to build a sourced
phrase from. Rather than draft a sentence from memory, we're dropping it — FINISH
as a marker gets covered as a Checkpoint task instead, working directly from the
source page's own examples.

## Family mission

At a shared meal — any meal, any location, doesn't have to be a restaurant — sign
HUNGRY, THIRSTY, and FINISH at the moments you'd normally say them out loud, instead
of saying them. Under 10 minutes, and it only takes one other person paying
attention.

Learning with a partner? `learners/shared/missions.md` has the two-person version of
this and every later unit's mission.

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Produce all 11 signs from this unit from memory, no notes, in under 90 seconds
- [ ] Correctly read all 11 signs when a partner signs them, or by replaying each
      sign's source page (video where the page has one, still images where it
      doesn't), no audio
- [ ] Watch the example sentences on the FINISH source page above, then produce one
      of them from memory and explain in one sentence what FINISH added to the
      meaning (every example there pairs FINISH with a verb — that's the
      completive-marker pattern, not a standalone word)
- [ ] Sign your order — using this unit's WATER/COFFEE/TEA/MILK/BREAD/CHICKEN/FISH
      vocabulary — to a family member role-playing server, no voicing. Optional
      stretch: try a real counter, but prep first per the etiquette article above
      (write your order out ahead of time, keep a backup copy, tip generously) —
      its described technique is pointing and writing, not this unit's signs, so
      treat a real-counter attempt as goodwill practice, not a pass/fail test
- [ ] Family mission logged in `journal.md`

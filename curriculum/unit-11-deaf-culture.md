# Unit 11 — Deaf Culture & Community

## Why this unit

Every sign in this curriculum belongs to the Deaf community first — you and your family
are borrowing it, not owning it, and this unit is where you learn to borrow well. It's
also the vocabulary that turns "I'm learning ASL" into an actual conversation: who's
Deaf, who's hearing, when you need an interpreter versus when you don't, what CULTURE
means when a Deaf person says it back to you. A small, deliberately tight vocabulary —
big consequence for getting it wrong.

## Watch

- https://www.handspeak.com/topic/220/ — "Deaf Culture and Sign Language," a Deaf-authored
  overview of what "Deaf culture" actually names and why language and culture are
  inseparable here. ~5 min read.
- https://www.handspeak.com/topic/230/ — "Hearing Allyship with Deaf People" — what the
  Deaf community asks of hearing learners, from the same site. ~8 min read.
- https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/topics/name-signs.htm — how name signs work in the
  Deaf community: what they are, who gives them, and why you don't pick your own.
  ~13 min read (longer than it looks — worth the time).
- https://www.nad.org/who-we-are/our-federation/state-association-affiliates/ — the
  National Association of the Deaf's actual state-by-state list of affiliated
  associations — how to find the actual Deaf community where you live. ~3 min.

## Learn

| sign | source | hook |
|---|---|---|
| DEAF | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/d/deaf.htm | the identity word, not a diagnosis — how the community names itself |
| HEARING | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/h/hearing.htm | how the Deaf community refers to people like you, if you're not Deaf |
| SIGN | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/s/sign.htm | the verb for what you've been doing all along — say this instead of "talk with hands" |
| LANGUAGE | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/l/language.htm | worth knowing, but optional after SIGN — the source explains why fluent signers usually let SIGN alone carry "sign language" |
| INTERPRETER | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/i/interpreter.htm | who you request at a medical appointment or job interview — not someone you're trying to replace |
| CULTURE | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/c/culture.htm | the word for everything this unit is actually about — shared history and norms, not just vocabulary |
| COMMUNITY | https://www.signingsavvy.com/search/community | the destination this whole tutor points toward — people, not flashcards |
| PROUD | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/p/proud.htm | how Deaf people describe their own identity — not the pity framing sometimes applied to deafness from outside the community |

## Drill

When this unit opens, `/lesson` appends to the deck:

- One `vocab` card per Learn row above (prompts: "Sign: DEAF", "Sign: HEARING",
  "Sign: SIGN", "Sign: LANGUAGE", "Sign: INTERPRETER", "Sign: CULTURE",
  "Sign: COMMUNITY", "Sign: PROUD"). 8 cards — kept deliberately small, on purpose.

No `fingerspell` or `phrase` cards this unit. The homework isn't more flashcards — it's
watching the Deaf-created media above and, per the Family mission below, spending real
time pointed at the Deaf community instead of this file.

## Family mission

With one other person, spend up to 10 minutes at
https://www.nad.org/who-we-are/our-federation/state-association-affiliates/ finding
your state's NAD-affiliated association. Don't sign up for anything yet; just find the
name and write it in the journal. This tutor's whole job is to make itself
unnecessary — this is the first step past it.

Learning with a partner? `learners/shared/missions.md` has the two-person version of
this and every later unit's mission.

## Checkpoint

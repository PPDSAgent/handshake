# Unit 02 — First Contact

## Why this unit

Unit 00 gave you HELLO, NAME, ME — enough to open a conversation. This unit gives you
enough to survive one: greeting someone new, answering yes or no, saying thanks and
sorry when either comes up, and closing out. AGAIN and SLOW matter more than anything
else here — they're what let you sit across from a fluent signer (family, a Deaf
neighbor, an interpreter) and keep the conversation going instead of nodding along
lost. By the end of this unit you can have a short, real, wordless exchange with
another person.

## Learn

| sign | source | hook |
|---|---|---|
| NICE | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/n/nice.htm | pairs with MEET-YOU as your opening line to someone new |
| MEET-YOU | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/m/meet.htm | say it once and you've had a real introduction, not just a wave |
| THANK-YOU | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/t/thank-you.htm | closes almost any exchange politely — use it more than feels natural at first |
| PLEASE | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/p/please.htm | how you ask for anything, including patience |
| SORRY | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/s/sorry.htm | for real apologies and small fumbles alike — you'll use it a lot while learning |
| YES | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/y/yes.htm | your half of every yes/no exchange |
| NO | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/n/no.htm | the other half — beginners under-use it out of politeness, don't |
| GOOD | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/g/good.htm | the default positive review, for food, weather, or a signed attempt |
| BAD | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/b/bad.htm | its negative pair, for when GOOD would be a lie |
| GOODBYE | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/g/goodbye.htm | the sign-off — bookends a conversation you opened with unit 00's HELLO |
| AGAIN | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/a/again.htm | your most useful sign as a beginner — ask for a repeat instead of guessing |
| SLOW | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/s/slow.htm | the other survival tool — the request that keeps a fluent signer's pace inside your reach |

## Drill

When this unit opens, `/lesson` appends to the deck:

- One `vocab` card per Learn row above (prompts: "Sign: NICE", "Sign: MEET-YOU",
  "Sign: THANK-YOU", "Sign: PLEASE", "Sign: SORRY", "Sign: YES", "Sign: NO",
  "Sign: GOOD", "Sign: BAD", "Sign: GOODBYE", "Sign: AGAIN", "Sign: SLOW"). 12 cards.
- One `phrase` card with an attested order: "Sign: NICE MEET-YOU" — it appears
  verbatim in Story 1 and the practice-sentence set at
  https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/lessons/lesson01.htm. AGAIN and SLOW are
  drilled as the standalone signs in the vocab cards above, not as a combined
  request phrase — no allowlist source attests a specific "PLEASE AGAIN" or "SLOW
  PLEASE" combination, and guessing at word order here would be exactly the kind of
  unsourced production claim Rule 1 exists to prevent. Mid-conversation, reach for
  AGAIN or SLOW on their own (see the family mission below).

## Family mission

Have a short, wordless, signed-only exchange with one other person, in person or on
a video call: greet them (HELLO, from unit 00), sign NICE MEET-YOU, ask them a
yes/no question so they can answer YES or NO, then close with THANK-YOU and GOODBYE.
If they sign back too fast or you lose the thread, use AGAIN or SLOW for real — that's
exactly what they're for, not just deck material. Under 5 minutes.

Learning with a partner? `learners/shared/missions.md` has the two-person version of
this and every later unit's mission.

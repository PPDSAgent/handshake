# Unit 04 — Numbers & Time

## Why this unit

Every plan runs on numbers and time — "call me at 3," "see you next WEEK," a phone
number read back correctly. Without this vocabulary a conversation stalls out after
introductions, because you can't actually schedule the next one. This unit gives you
the counting system from 1–20 (what covers prices, quantities, clock times, and
dates) and the words for day, week, month, and year, so you can set a real time to
talk with the person you're learning this for and mean it.

## Learn

| sign | source | hook |
|---|---|---|

| 11–20 | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/pages-signs/n/numbers11-20.htm | the second ten — clock times, dates, anything just past a full ten |

## Drill

When this unit opens, `/lesson` appends to the deck:

- One `vocab` card per number 1–20 (prompts "Sign: 1" … "Sign: 20"), source =
  numbers1-10.htm for 1–10, numbers11-20.htm for 11–20. 20 cards, but they enter
  play range-by-range: cards from a range not yet taught stay `due` = the day
  that range's session runs (same gating as unit 01's letter clusters).
- One `vocab` card per remaining Learn row (prompts "Sign: TIME", "Sign: DAY",
  "Sign: WEEK", "Sign: MONTH", "Sign: YEAR", "Sign: TODAY", "Sign: TOMORROW",
  "Sign: YESTERDAY"). 8 cards.
- Two `phrase` cards that force the point of the unit: "Phrase: use TODAY or
  TOMORROW plus a number to propose a real time to talk with someone" and "Phrase:
  use DAY or WEEK plus a number to set a real day for your next session." Both
  answer-keyed to the Learn-row sources above — there's no single source page for
  a combined phrase, so grade these on whether the plan actually got made.

## Family mission

Call or video-call a family member and agree, in ASL, on a day and a rough time to
talk again: sign a day word (DAY, TODAY, TOMORROW, or WEEK) plus a number for the
hour. Under 10 minutes, and it ends with an actual plan on the calendar.

If they don't know any ASL yet, that's a separate, smaller mission for this week:
teach them just the one number they need to read back to you (e.g. the hour you're
proposing), not the full 1–12 set — save teaching the rest for a later call.

Learning with a partner? `learners/shared/missions.md` has the two-person version of
this and every later unit's mission.

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Sign 1–20 in sequence from memory, no chart, under 90 seconds
- [ ] Read 8 of 10 random numbers (1–20) signed back to you correctly
- [ ] TODAY, TOMORROW, and YESTERDAY each correctly identified out of order by
      another person, 3 of 3
- [ ] Combine a time word with a number to correctly propose a real day and time
      in ASL, confirmed readable by another person
- [ ] Family mission completed and logged in the journal (this is where the actual
      plan gets made — see Family mission above)

# Unit 07 — Feelings & Health

## Why this unit

Two people who can only trade facts aren't having a conversation — they need a way to
say how something landed, whether they're up for what's next, and what's wrong when
something's wrong. This unit adds that layer: enough vocabulary to answer "how are
you" with more than a shrug, and to handle the ordinary logistics of getting sick,
getting better, and asking for help along the way. It also introduces the three signs
every ASL learner needs early and needs right: DEAF, HARD-OF-HEARING, and HEARING —
the words for who's in the room and how they access language. Unit 11 is where those
three get their full context; this unit just makes sure you have them before you need
them.

## Watch

- https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/lessons/lesson19.htm — Lifeprint's feelings lesson:
  FEEL, HAPPY, TIRED, ANGRY and related vocabulary in context (~10 min read-through).
- https://www.youtube.com/billvicars — search "feelings" or "health" on Dr. Vicars'
  channel for the signed versions at natural speed; the chart-and-lesson pages
  undersell how these move (~10 min).
- https://www.nad.org/ — the National Association of the Deaf's homepage. Not a
  lesson, just a first look at who and what DEAF/HARD-OF-HEARING/HEARING refer to as
  identities and communities, not just vocabulary words. Unit 11 goes deep here; today
  is reconnaissance (~5 min).

## Drill

When this unit opens, `/lesson` appends to the deck:

- One `vocab` card per Learn row above (prompts: "Sign: HAPPY", "Sign: SAD", "Sign:
  ANGRY", "Sign: TIRED", "Sign: SICK", "Sign: HURT", "Sign: DOCTOR", "Sign:
  MEDICINE", "Sign: FEEL", "Sign: DEAF", "Sign: HARD-OF-HEARING", "Sign:
  HEARING"). 12 cards.
- One `phrase` card with an attested order: "Sign: HOW YOU FEEL?" — glossed verbatim
  as practice item 11.B/06 at https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/lessons/lesson11.htm
  and again as "TELL-me HOW YOU FEEL" at
  https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/lessons/lesson10.htm. Note the WH-sign leads here,
  the way lesson 5 says it does — not the English "how do you feel."
- One `phrase` card that is a **fluency drill, not an attested sentence**: "Sign: ME
  DEAF" or "Sign: ME HEARING", whichever matches the learner (ask, don't assume). The
  DEAF and HEARING pages above teach each sign; neither attests this two-sign pairing,
  so treat it as a production run and let the lesson videos show you how identity is
  actually framed in conversation.

## Family mission

Video or in-person, under 10 minutes: take turns asking HOW YOU FEEL and answering
with one sign from this unit — no English, no repeats allowed until you've each used
every sign once. Whoever breaks first (speaks, fingerspells around a gap, or repeats
early) resets the round.

Learning with a partner? `learners/shared/missions.md` has the two-person version of
this and every other unit's mission.

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Produce all 12 Learn signs from memory, no source page open, under 90 seconds
- [ ] Correctly answer HOW YOU FEEL with a different sign three times in a row
      without repeating
- [ ] Sign DOCTOR and MEDICINE in the same short exchange without hesitating between
      them
- [ ] Explain in one sentence what the NAD Watch link says the word "Deaf" includes
      on that site (meta-knowledge check, sourced from the Watch link — not invented)
- [ ] Family mission round completed with a partner, logged in the journal

# Unit 01 — The Alphabet Protocol

## Why this unit

Fingerspelling is ASL's ASCII: 26 handshapes that can encode *any* English word. It's
maybe 10% of everyday signing, but it's the bootstrap layer — from the day you're
comfortable with it, you can communicate anything (slowly) while the rest of your
vocabulary grows. It's also the part of ASL that machines can read, which is what the
webcam lab (Phase 3) will exploit. Master this and everything else compiles.

## Watch

- https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/fingerspelling/fingerspelling.htm — the core
  reference: alphabet chart, lessons, and practice guidance. Your home base for this
  whole unit (~15 min to survey; return often).
- https://www.youtube.com/billvicars — Dr. Vicars' channel; search it for
  "fingerspelling lesson" and watch lesson 1 (~10 min). Seeing the letters signed at
  human speed matters — the chart alone undersells the rhythm.
- https://asl.ms/ — the practice machine you'll live in: it spells words at you at a
  speed you choose, you type what you read. Bookmark it now.

## Learn

The 26 letters, worked in confusion-cluster groups — not alphabetical order. All
groups source from the same reference: the fingerspelling page above. Six rows here
is deliberately below the usual 8–15: each row is a *cluster*, so the real load is
all 26 letters, grouped by which ones you'll mix up rather than by the alphabet.

| sign | source | hook |
|---|---|---|
| A S T M N E | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/fingerspelling/fingerspelling.htm | the cluster beginners confuse most — drill as a set until they separate |
| B C O F D | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/fingerspelling/fingerspelling.htm | high-frequency and distinct — quick wins |
| L Y I J | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/fingerspelling/fingerspelling.htm | this cluster ends with a letter the chart alone can't show you — the video explains why |
| G H P Q K | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/fingerspelling/fingerspelling.htm | the ones that make you check the chart twice; P/K and G/Q are related pairs |
| R U V W X | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/fingerspelling/fingerspelling.htm | the cluster that trips readers up at speed — R/U/V especially, drill them together |
| Z | https://www.lifeprint.com/asl101/fingerspelling/fingerspelling.htm | the other letter the chart undersells — the video makes it click in seconds |

Session pacing that works: one cluster per session, previous clusters reviewed via
`/drill` first. Six sessions ≈ six clusters, then the checkpoint.

## Drill

When this unit opens, `/lesson` appends to the deck:

- One `fingerspell` card per letter, A–Z (prompt "Fingerspell: A", …), source = the
  fingerspelling page. 26 cards, but they enter play cluster-by-cluster: cards from
  clusters not yet taught stay `due` = the day their cluster's session runs.
- Three `fingerspell` word cards: "Fingerspell: your full name", "Fingerspell: your
  street name", "Fingerspell: COFFEE" (double letters need a technique — the source
  page covers it; watch for it).

Receptive practice is session-time, not card-time: every `/drill` session in this
unit ends with 3 minutes at https://asl.ms/ — start slow, raise speed only at ≥80%
accuracy, record the speed setting in the journal.

## Family mission

Video call with a family member: fingerspell your name to them, then teach them to
fingerspell *their* name back to you. Reading someone else's hands is a different
skill than producing — this mission trains both, and it recruits your first practice
partner.

## Checkpoint

- [ ] Produce A–Z from memory, no chart, under 60 seconds
- [ ] Read 4–5 letter words at asl.ms at a middle speed setting with ≥80% accuracy
      (log the exact speed — it's your baseline for unit 12's exit retest)
- [ ] Fingerspell your full name smoothly enough that a stranger could read it
- [ ] Read a family member's name signed to you on a video call
- [ ] Explain the double-letter technique in one sentence (meta-knowledge check)
